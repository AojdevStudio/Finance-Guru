## Judge the trailing twelve months

The canonical metric for a Layer 2 fund is trailing 12-month yield, not the latest monthly distribution. A single down month inside the green band below is normal variance. It is not a sell signal.

Compare a fund with funds of similar yield. Do not judge a 20% yield fund by the month-to-month stability of a 4% bond.

## Three classes

Classify before you judge the variance. A −9% month on a Class 3 fund can be normal. The same month on a Class 1 fund is a reason to look.

| Class | Vehicle | Premium share of distributions | Normal monthly variance | Trailing 12-month yield | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Options-premium / covered-call ETFs | 0.60–0.80 | ±5–10% | 7–10% | JEPI, JEPQ, QQQI, SPYI, QQQY |
| 2 | Modern closed-end funds | Mixed: premiums, dividends, bonds, gains; often levered | ±5–15% | 12–22% | ECAT, CLM, CRF, BDJ, ETY, ETV |
| 3 | Volatility-harvesting single-stock funds | 0.80–0.95 | ±10–25% | 60–85% | YMAX, MSTY, AMZY |

Yield and variance move together:

| Yield band | Expected monthly variance | Vehicle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2–4% | ±1–2% | Bonds and dividend aristocrats (outside Layer 2) |
| 7–10% | ±5–7% | Class 1 covered-call ETFs |
| 12–22% | ±5–15% | Class 2 modern closed-end funds |
| 20–30% | ±10–20% | High-yield closed-end funds and preferred stock |
| 60–85% | ±10–30% | Class 3 single-stock option funds |

A fund whose variance sits outside the band for its yield is a data-quality flag. Say so, and do not force a class.

## Flags

### Green — no action

The month is inside the class band above. Monitor trailing 12-month yield. Do not recommend a sale.

### Yellow — investigate

- Two or three consecutive months of 10–15% declines.
- Trailing 12-month yield down 15–20%.
- NAV falling at the same time as the distribution.

Pause new purchases, read the issuer's notice, and compare peers. Resume only when the cause is a temporary market condition rather than a strategy change.

### Red — sell trigger

- A sustained decline above 30% over three months.
- A single-month drop above 50%.
- NAV eroding faster than the distribution (return-of-capital spiral).
- The manager announces a strategy change, a fee increase, or a forced deleveraging.

A red flag is the only sell trigger. A cut above 50% is immediate. A cut of 30–50% is a pause and a 30-day watch. NAV stability matters more than a smooth distribution: a stable NAV with a variable payout is the product working; a falling NAV with a still-high payout is the product consuming itself.

Separate option premium, dividends, capital gains, and return of capital. Each has its own tax treatment. Do not label the whole distribution a dividend.
